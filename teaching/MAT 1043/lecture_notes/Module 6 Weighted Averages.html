Motivation

When computing averages in the past, we made the assumption that every data point had the exact same worth, or contribution, to the pool. Many things in life are not as such, instead we need a way to find an average while showing respect to differing levels of importance.

Revisiting Averages and Weights

In Module 2, we defined the average as the sum of all data values divided by the total number of data points, or simply

\[ \text{Average} = \frac{\text{Sum of Values}}{\# \text{ of Values}}. \]

We also defined in Module 1 the idea of a weight, the percentage of importance which is multiplied to a value to scale it.

Weighted Averages

We now combine the average and weights.

Definition (Weighed Average).

An average where each data point is scaled by its importance taken over the sum of the weights.

Our process of computing a weighted average is as follows.

  1. Scale each data point by its weight
  2. Find the sum of the scaled data
  3. Find the sum of the weights
  4. Divide the sum of the scaled data by the sum of the weights

Examples

Below are some examples of computing weighted averages with a brief aside about UT San Antonio's GPA system,

Example 1.

Suppose we are taking ART 1012 - Introduction to Handbuilding and we finish the class with the following scores with their respective part of the final grade.

Course Results
Category Score Part of Grade
Homework 80 40%
Projects 100 35%
Exams 90 25%

We want to compute our final grade as a weighted average,

First, we will scale each score by its part of the grade.

For Homework

\[ 80 (0.40) = 32\]

For Projects

\[100 (0.35) = 35 \]

For Exams

\[90(0.25) = 22.5 \]

Second, we will find the sum of the above weighted scores

\[ 32 + 35 + 22.5 = 89.5\]

Third, we find the sum of the weights

\[40 \% + 35 \% + 25 \% = 100 \% \quad (\text{or 1.00}) \]

Lastly, we divide our sum of scores (89.5) by the sum of our weights (1)

\[ \text{Weighted Average} = \frac{89.5}{1} = 89.5 \]

Hence we would pass the class with a \( B^+ \).

Example (Calculating GPA).

Suppose we have just finished our first semester and want to calculate our GPA according to UT San Antonio's GPA Scheme. Below are the results of our first semester,

Course Grades
Course Letter Grade GPA Scale
MAT 1043 \( A^+ \) 4.0
HIS 1044 \( B \) 3.0
PSY 1063 \( C\) 2.0
WRC 1022 \( B^- \) 2.67

We follow our process for finding the weighted average, identifying our data values by the GPA scale and our weights by the credit hours.

Scaling our data

MAT 1043: \( 4.0 (3) = 12 \) 
HIS 1044: \( 3.0 (4) = 12 \) 
PSY 1063: \( 2.0 (3) = 6 \) 
WRC 1022: \( 2.67 (2) = 5.34 \) 

Summing our weighted values

\[ 12 + 12 + 6 + 5.34 = 35.34\]

Summing our weights (Credits)

\[ 3 + 4 + 3 + 2 = 12 \]

Finding our weighted average

\[ \text{GPA} = \frac{35.34}{12} = 2..945. \]

Note.

UT San Antonio rounds down to the second decimal point, hence our true GPA would be 2.94.
