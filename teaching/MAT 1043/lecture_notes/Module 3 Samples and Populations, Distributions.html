Motivation

Polling individuals one-by-one takes far too long to be effective, instead we wish to take a small group and make generalizations about the entire group.

Populations and Samples

For our purposes, we create the following definitions

Definition (Population).

A group that we wish to make generalizations about.

We can consider the population as Texans.

Definition (Sample).

A subset of a population.

In the population of Texans, we think of San Antonians, Austinites, and Hustonians as some samples.

Representative Samples

A sample can be a single person, obviously this isn't a great representation of a large group, so we consider ways to make a sample reflective of the entire population.

Definition (Representative Sample).

A sample is said to be representative (or descriptive), if the sample has little bias and a good sample size.

We define the terms used below

Definition (Bias).

Systematic issues created by the selection of the sample, e.g., asking only Austinites about Texas,

And our other factor

Definition (Sample Size).

How many people asked, less people, less variety of opinions.

Now that we have our vocabulary, we look at a more interesting example.

Example.

Suppose one hundred senior UT San Antonio students are polled about their feelings towards Dr. Pepper. Out of the students polled 75% of the students respond favorably.

Dissecting the example we find the following.

  • Population: UT San Antonio Students
  • Sample: Senior Students
  • Sample Size: 100
  • Is the sample representative? No
  • The question asked: Is Dr. Pepper good?

Despite this sample being non-representative of the entire student population, we can gleam some understanding that some Seniors have a preference towards Dr. Pepper.
